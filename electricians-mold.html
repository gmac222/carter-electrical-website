Common Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Is an EICR safety inspection mandatory before selling a property in Mold?

While not legally mandatory for private home sellers, mortgage lenders and conveyancing solicitors in Flintshire increasingly request an up-to-date EICR. Having a satisfactory report avoids conveyancing delays and prevents buyers from negotiating price reductions due to outdated wiring.

What are the main warning signs that a property in Mold requires a consumer unit upgrade?

Key indicators include old rewireable fuse boxes with cast-iron or wooden backings, lack of residual current device (RCD) protection, flickering lights, or scorched socket outlets. Upgrading to a modern metal RCBO board with Surge Protection (SPD) ensures full BS 7671 safety compliance.

What electrical regulations apply to private landlords in Flintshire under Welsh law?

Under the Renting Homes (Wales) Act and electrical safety regulations, landlords must ensure fixed wiring is inspected at least every 5 years, accompanied by an EICR certificate. Landlords must also ensure working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are hardwired on every floor.

Can Carter Electrical carry out commercial retail fit-outs on Mold High Street?

Yes. We work with local retailers, cafes, and offices in Mold town centre and surrounding commercial parks. We design energy-efficient LED lighting schemes, install emergency lighting to BS 5266, and perform commercial power distribution fit-outs.

How do I know if my electrician in CH7 is fully registered and accredited?

You can verify registration on the Registered Competent Person Electrical register. Carter Electrical is a fully NICEIC-approved contractor. All our work is self-certified with Building Control and backed by the NICEIC Platinum Promise warranty.

Which villages surrounding Mold do your domestic electricians cover?

We cover Mold (CH7) and surrounding areas including Gwernymynydd, Sychdyn, Mynydd Isa, Buckley, New Brighton, Loggerheads, and Llanferres.
